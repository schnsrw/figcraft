S5

in progress

Gate: a fenced multi-replica relay passes crash, revocation, compatibility and load tests.

Distributed relay: leases, routing and crash safety

Room ownership is ours: leases in PostgreSQL next to the fencing tokens, rather than Plane or Rivet. Most of it is now built and tested; two items and the gate remain.

How it works

Any relay accepts a connection and passes the client on to the room's current owner, so no client or protocol change is needed. A relay that restarts takes its own stale lease back at once. Draining commits and snapshots rooms, releases their leases and tells clients to reconnect. No Redis dependency for ownership.

Built and tested so far

Leases with renewal, loss and release; owner routing; SIGTERM drain; warm idle rooms and scheduled snapshots; six bounded quotas; durable client ids; realtime tickets through the owner path; and tests that SIGKILL the real process before and after a commit. Rooms commit concurrently: about 1,000 commits per second per relay at millisecond latency, where one-at-a-time commits reached 570 to 799 at 100 ms or more.

Still open

Large images through verified asset references, which needs editor support as well as the relay, and the gate: one full run of the multi-replica, crash, revocation, compatibility and load tests with no acknowledged edit lost.

Why

Lowest effort and no new dependency to maintain. Fencing tokens already guarantee a single writer even during a partition, and commit-before-broadcast, snapshot recovery and quarantine already exist.

Alternatives considered

Rivet's scalable self-hosted storage needs FoundationDB under an enterprise licence. Plane (MIT) fits the model, but its long-term maintenance is uncertain.

Already in place before this work

Fencing tokens, snapshot-plus-replay restore, commit-before-broadcast, quarantine of unrecoverable rooms, compatibility with existing servers and desktop sessions, and per-client admission.
